{% load textstyle %} {% comment %} The "Picked for you" bar. Three states, and the difference between them is the whole point of the component: 1. No profile (signed out, or signed in with an empty survey) — one honest line and a way to fix it. Never cards. A bar labelled "picked for you" shown to someone we know nothing about is a lie that costs exactly the trust this page exists to earn. 2. A profile, but nothing clears the score threshold — say so plainly. The bar is allowed to be empty; padding it with the least-bad six would make a full bar meaningless on the days it is genuinely full. 3. Picks — grouped by firm, each group carrying the reasons that put it there. WHY GROUPED, AND WHY THE REASONS SIT WHERE THEY DO. Three of the scorer's four axes (tier, track, region) read FIRM properties, so two roles at one firm cannot differ on them. One flat card per role therefore printed the same "why" string six times over three firms — the eye landed on four chips that were constant and slid past the role title, deadline and location, which were the only things that actually varied. `_group_picks` in directory/views.py now lifts each reason to the broadest level at which it is literally true: bar head -> reasons identical on every pick firm head -> reasons identical on every role at that firm role row -> whatever is left Nothing is dropped and nothing is reworded — a reason prints exactly once, at its own level, with the scorer's own sentence. The ranking is untouched. Every reason's long form is BOTH the chip's `title` and the text inside the "What these mean" disclosure, because a `title` tooltip is mouse-only and the class-year admission — that a year was inferred from a programme year rather than stated by the posting — is the one sentence on this page that must never be reachable by hover alone. Layout note: this is a wrapping grid, not a scrolling rail. The rail it replaced overflowed its own box (1392px of room, 1761px of content) with no affordance saying so; a grid that wraps has no hidden content to signpost. Blocks are min-height, never fixed, and nothing here sets `overflow: hidden` — two previous bugs on this page were content clipped by a box smaller than its contents, so a block that needs more room grows instead. {% endcomment %}
